### Lesson 1 [ai, lesson]: Tokens and context window
**Ayush** (2026-11-05 09:47): So what does it mean when we say “a billion parameters” in this context?
**Tutor** (2026-11-05 09:47): A **parameter** is a learned number inside the model—roughly like a dial the model adjusts during training. A model with **a billion parameters** has about a billion such numbers; together, they shape how it processes tokens and predicts what comes next.

Parameters are different from **tokens**: tokens are pieces of text the model reads or writes, while parameters are part of the model itself. The number of parameters also isn’t the context-window size; that limits how many tokens the model can handle at once.
**Ayush** (2026-11-05 09:47): So what does it mean when we say “a billion parameters” in this context?
**Tutor** (2026-11-05 09:47): It means the model contains about **one billion learned numerical values**—often called weights. During training, these values are adjusted so the model gets better at predicting the next token. At runtime, the model uses them to process the tokens you provide and generate a reply.

They aren’t a billion words or facts, and they don’t determine how many tokens fit in the context window. A useful interview distinction: **parameters are the model’s learned internals; tokens are its input and output units; the context window is how many tokens it can handle at once.**
