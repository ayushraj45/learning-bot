# Coding challenge: Month-over-month revenue growth (SQL)

## Problem
You have a table `orders`:

| column | type |
|---|---|
| order_id | int |
| customer_id | int |
| order_date | date |
| amount | numeric(10,2) |
| status | text ('paid', 'refunded', 'pending') |

Write a query that returns, for each calendar month in 2026, the total **paid** revenue, the previous month's revenue, and the percentage growth versus the previous month (rounded to 1 decimal place). Also return a running total of paid revenue across 2026 up to and including that month.

Columns: `month` (first day of the month), `revenue`, `prev_revenue`, `growth_pct`, `ytd_revenue`.

## Example
Paid revenue by month: Jan 1000, Feb 1500, Mar 1200.

| month | revenue | prev_revenue | growth_pct | ytd_revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-01 | 1000.00 | NULL | NULL | 1000.00 |
| 2026-02-01 | 1500.00 | 1000.00 | 50.0 | 2500.00 |
| 2026-03-01 | 1200.00 | 1500.00 | -20.0 | 3700.00 |

## Constraints
- Only `status = 'paid'` orders in 2026.
- January has no previous month: growth must be NULL, not an error.
- A month with zero previous revenue must not cause a divide-by-zero error.
- Months with no paid orders can be absent from the output (mention in your own notes how you'd handle them if they had to appear).
- Postgres-style SQL is fine.

## Hints
<details>
<summary>Hints</summary>

1. Aggregate to one row per month first (a CTE), then apply window functions on top of that.
2. `LAG(col) OVER (ORDER BY ...)` gives the previous row's value.
3. For growth, think about what `NULLIF` does to a zero denominator.
4. Running total: think about the frame, and whether ties can occur here after aggregation.
</details>
